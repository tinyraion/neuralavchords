Neural Audio-Visual Chords (NAVC): A Multimodal Framework for Temporally and Spatially Aligned Sound Generation from Visual Cues
Christian Simon, Wei-Yao Wang, Dongseok Shim, Kazuki Shimada, Masato Ishii, Takashi Shibuya, Shusuke Takahashi, Yuki Mitsufuji
Abstract

We introduce Neural Audio‑Visual Chords (NAVC), a multimodal generative framework for video‑to‑audio synthesis that aligns sound with visual content across temporal, spatial, and semantic dimensions. Inspired by the cinematic notion of audiovisual chords, our approach models sound as a structured response to evolving visual cues rather than purely global information. To this end, the framework integrates a diverse set of visual conditioning signals, including audio–visual sound event trajectories, localized and global semantic embeddings, depth cues, and textual descriptions. These unified cues are fused through an enhanced video‑to‑audio architecture equipped with Mixture‑of‑Experts (MoE) routing, enabling adaptive specialization across different alignment factors. This design facilitates fine‑grained temporal synchronization and controllable sound placement, supporting diegetic audio generation within a unified model. We demonstrate the effectiveness of Neural Audio‑Visual Chords on stereo audio-visual benchmark datasets such as Sphere360 and YT‑Ambigen, showing improved alignment and expressiveness compared to prior video‑to‑audio approaches. Finally, we discuss how the proposed architecture naturally extends to more general multi‑conditioning audio generation settings, opening avenues for scalable and controllable audiovisual synthesis.
